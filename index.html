Get started

    
Set Up Your Finances

    
The decisions that matter most in your first weeks of service, in priority order. Most take under 15 minutes.

  

  

  

    

      
If your pay is late or wrong

      

        
Rule You're paid on the 1st and 15th; if that's a weekend or holiday, the workday before. Your first payment after entering service can take several weeks to arrive and may come as a lump sum.

        
    
          
  • Compare your  with your orders and report errors to finance in writing, through your chain.
    • 
          
  • If you'll run short, ask finance about an emergency partial payment of pay you've already earned.
    • 
          
  • Don't spend money you haven't received yet: back pay, reimbursements or a first BAH payment.
    • 
          
  • You repay overpayments even when they're the government's error. Ask before spending an unexpected deposit.
    • 
        

      

    

    

      
Avoid these traps

      

        
    
          
  • A big car payment. Keep payment, insurance, gas and upkeep near 10–15% of take-home pay.
    • 
          
  • Renting to your BAH.  is a ceiling, not a target. Utilities and savings come out of it too.
    • 
          
  • High-cost credit. Payday loans, pawn shops and carried card balances. The  caps most consumer credit at 36% APR, but not car loans secured by the car.
    • 
          
  • Commission-based "advice." Compare any investment or insurance pitch with the TSP and SGLI first.
    • 
          
  • Allotment-only sellers. DoD has banned new allotments to buy, lease or rent personal property since 2015.
    • 
        

        
Short on cash in an emergency? The Air & Space Forces Aid Society (and each service's relief society) offers interest-free loans and grants.

Compares everything you'd pay to own (including buying and selling costs, minus what you'd get back at sale) with what you'd pay to rent. Growth rates are assumptions, not forecasts. Excludes tax effects and rental income.

Before you decide to buy

      
    
        
  • Assignment length. Buying and selling often cost 8–10% of the price combined. A 2–4 year tour gives little time to recover that.
    • 
        
  • Relocation risk. Orders can come early. Could you sell quickly, or rent it out and still cover the mortgage through a vacancy?
    • 
        
  • Upfront cash. Even with a VA loan and no down payment, you'll need closing costs, moving costs and repairs.
    • 
        
  • Maintenance. Owners pay for every repair. Renters call the landlord.
    • 
        
  • Emergency savings. Keep your emergency fund whole after closing.
    • 
        
  • BAH isn't a mortgage plan. It stops covering this home when you move, and lenders' approval amounts are maximums, not recommendations.

Renters and homeowners insurance explained
  

    
A landlord's policy covers the building, not your belongings or your liability. If you rent, you need renters insurance; if you own, homeowners insurance (and your lender will require it). Prices vary widely by location, property, insurer, coverage limits and deductible, so compare real quotes.

    

      
CoverageRenters insuranceHomeowners insurance

      
        
The buildingNot covered; that's the landlord's policyDwelling coverage pays to repair or rebuild the home. Insure to rebuilding cost, which can differ from the sale price.

        
Your belongingsPersonal property coverage, up to your limitPersonal property coverage, usually a share of the dwelling limit

        
LiabilityIf someone is hurt in your home or you damage others' propertySame, usually with higher limits; consider an umbrella policy later

        
Living elsewhereAdditional living expenses if a covered loss makes your home unlivable, when the policy includes itSame

        
Deductible and limitsYou pay the deductible on each claim; the insurer pays up to the limit. A higher deductible lowers the premium but raises what you pay after a loss. Check whether belongings are covered at replacement cost or actual cash value, and the sub-limits for jewelry, electronics or firearms.

        
Common exclusionsFlood and earthquake are usually excluded and need separate policies or endorsements. Wear and tear, pests and lack of maintenance aren't covered. Some policies limit coverage for property in storage or in transit during a move.

Not the same thing: your mortgage payment repays the loan (principal and interest); property tax goes to the local government; homeowners insurance protects the home and your liability. Lenders often collect tax and insurance through an escrow account, so a quoted "payment" may or may not include them. Also review auto insurance whenever you move, since rates follow where the car is garaged, and confirm your SGLI coverage and beneficiaries.

    Common questions
    
    
    
      
    Are BAH and BAS taxed?
    Rule No. They're excluded from federal income tax and payroll taxes and don't appear in Box 1 of your W-2. Basic pay is taxable. That's why a civilian job needs a higher salary to match your take-home pay, and why lenders treat allowances differently.
    
      
    Do I keep BAH I don't spend on rent?
    Rule Yes. BAH is set by duty location, grade and dependency status, not by what you pay. It's protected from decreasing while you stay at the same location with the same grade and status. Without dependents in government quarters, you don't receive full BAH.
    
      
    How much should I spend on rent?
    Guidance Start from your budget, not a percentage. The housing step compares three limits (what your budget leaves, BAH minus utilities and insurance, and 30% of gross pay) and recommends a range at or below the lowest.
    
      
    How big should my emergency fund be?
    Guidance Start with $1,000–$2,000, then build to three months of essential expenses in a separate, federally insured savings account. Before a move, set aside its upfront costs separately so the move doesn't drain your emergency fund.
    
      
    Do I need renters insurance if my landlord has insurance?
    Yes. The landlord's policy covers the building, not your belongings or your liability if someone is injured in your home. Many leases, including privatized base housing, require renters insurance.
    
      
    Does homeowners insurance cover floods?
    Usually not. Flood (and often earthquake) coverage is separate. Lenders require flood insurance for homes in certain flood zones, and it's worth considering elsewhere. Check before you buy, since it can change your monthly cost significantly.
    
      
    Should I start the TSP before the match begins?
    Guidance Yes. Money invested in your first two years has the longest time to grow, and the habit is easier to set early. The TSP projection shows the cost of waiting.
    
      
    What should I do about student loans?
    • Rule Active-duty service counts toward Public Service Loan Forgiveness on federal Direct Loans (120 qualifying payments).
    • Rule The SCRA caps interest at 6% on loans from before active duty. Federal servicers usually apply it automatically; private lenders need a written request with orders.
    • Rule Federal repayment plans changed in 2025–2026 and the SAVE plan has ended. Check studentaid.gov before choosing a plan.
    
      
    Is a car affordable on my pay?
    Guidance A common test: at least 20% down, a loan of 4 years or less, and total car costs (payment, insurance, gas, upkeep) under 10% of gross income. The Military Lending Act doesn't cover a loan secured by the car you're buying, so compare dealer financing with your own bank or credit union.
    
      
    Are premium credit cards with military fee waivers worth it?
    Guidance Only once you reliably pay every card in full. Issuer fee waivers are company policy, not a legal right, and can change. Rewards never outweigh interest on a carried balance.
    
      
    Is there a uniform allowance?
    Rule Officers can receive a one-time initial uniform allowance of up to $400 and an additional allowance of up to $200 on entering active duty. Neither covers a full set of uniforms, so budget for the rest. Enlisted members receive an initial clothing issue and an annual clothing replacement allowance instead.
    
      
    What financial training is required at my first duty station?
    Rule Federal law (10 U.S.C. §992) requires financial literacy training at set points, including arrival at your first duty station, TSP vesting, continuation pay, and events such as marriage or a first child. Your Military & Family Readiness Center runs it.
    
      
    Where can I get free, confidential help?
    • Personal Financial Counselor at your base Military & Family Readiness Center.
    • Military OneSource: 800-342-9647 (free counseling and MilTax tax filing).
    • Base legal office: leases, contracts, SCRA, wills.
    • Air & Space Forces Aid Society: emergency interest-free loans and grants.
